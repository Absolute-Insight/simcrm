# What Vectora is

Vectora is a CRM for a sales team that would rather be told what needs doing
than go looking for it.

Most CRMs are a filing cabinet with a search box. You open one, and it shows you
a list. Which of those forty deals is quietly dying? That is your problem to
work out, every morning, from memory.

Vectora starts from the other end. It watches your pipeline continuously and
puts what it finds in one place — the **suggestion inbox** — with the reason
attached. A deal has gone quiet. A close date is a fortnight away and the stage
says it will not be met. A lead's first-response clock has run out. Each of
those is a row you can act on in a click, or dismiss, and dismissing teaches it
to leave you alone about that kind of thing for longer.

## The parts

| | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Suggestions** | The day's worklist. Ranked, with the evidence attached. |
| **Planner** | What you intend to do this week, resolved against what you actually did. |
| **Dashboard** | Where things stand — different for a rep and for a manager. |
| **Reports** | Six built-in reports, exportable and printable. |
| **Leads, Deals, Contacts, Organizations, Tasks, Notes, Calls** | The records themselves. |

## What runs without a model

Almost all of it. The signals, deal health, the planner, forecasting, reports,
digests and automation rules are ordinary code — they compute the same answer
every time and they work with the assistant switched off, which is how Vectora
ships.

The assistant is a separate, optional tier: it drafts replies and summarises
email threads. It talks to any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, including a model
running on your own hardware, so nothing has to leave your network. See
[The assistant](/crm/help/assistant).

## A note on this help centre

It is served by Vectora itself, from the same container as the rest of the
product. There is nothing to reach on the internet, and no page here describes
a feature this build does not have.
