## What it is

The body material sets the pressure–temperature envelope and the resistance to corrosion from outside and inside; the **trim** — seats, discs, stems, balls and other wetted moving parts — is what actually wears and seals, and is often a different, harder or more corrosion-resistant material than the body. Elastomers and plastics appear as seats, liners, sleeves and packing and usually set the temperature limit of the whole valve.

## Typical sizes and ratings

Common body materials, their cast (C) and forged (F) grades, and where they fit:

- **Cast iron / ductile iron** (BS EN 1561, EN 1563): water, low-pressure air, PN 10–16; ductile iron is far tougher and is the standard for water works valves. Not for steam above low pressure or for hydrocarbons in most plant specifications.
- **Carbon steel** — WCB cast (ASTM A216), A105 forged: general process, steam and hydrocarbons to about 425 °C. **LCB / LF2** for low temperature to -46 °C.
- **Chrome-moly** — WC6 / F11, WC9 / F22: high-temperature steam to about 550 °C in power plants.
- **Stainless** — CF8 / F304, CF8M / F316 (ASTM A351 / A182): corrosion resistance, food and chemical service, cryogenic. 316 is the default for anything with chlorides or acids.
- **Duplex and super duplex** (CD3MN / F51, CE3MN / F53): seawater, brine, high-chloride mine water, higher strength than austenitic stainless.
- **Alloy 20, Hastelloy C, Monel, titanium:** sulphuric acid, wet chlorine, hydrochloric acid and other aggressive chemistries.
- **Bronze and brass:** small utility valves, potable water, marine; limited pressure and temperature.

## Materials

API 600 and API 602 trim numbers are the shorthand for steel valve internals: trim 1 (13 Cr throughout), trim 5 (hard-faced Stellite seat and disc), trim 8 (13 Cr with hard-faced seats), trim 10 (316 stainless), trim 12 (316 with Stellite seats), trim 16 (316 with Monel). Hard facing is used above about 400 °C, on steam, and wherever wire-drawing or galling is a risk.

Soft materials:
- **EPDM:** water, steam to about 120 °C, mild chemicals; not oils.
- **NBR (nitrile):** oils, hydrocarbons, to about 90 °C.
- **FKM (Viton):** higher temperature and aggressive chemicals; poor with steam and ketones.
- **Natural rubber and polyurethane:** abrasion, slurry sleeves and liners.
- **PTFE / RPTFE / PFA:** near-universal chemical resistance, seats to about 200 °C, liners for corrosives; cold-flows under load.
- **PEEK:** high-temperature, high-pressure seats.

## Where it is used

- **Water and potable reticulation:** ductile iron bodies, EPDM seats, potable-water-approved coatings and elastomers.
- **Mining slurry and tailings:** ductile iron or steel bodies, natural rubber or polyurethane liners, hard-chromed or overlaid gates.
- **Petrochemical and refining:** WCB / A105 with 13 Cr or Stellite trim; sour service to NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 where H₂S is present.
- **Power generation and steam:** WC6 / WC9 bodies, Stellite trim, graphite packing.
- **Food and beverage, pulp and paper:** 316 stainless, FDA-listed elastomers, polished finishes.
- **Fire protection:** ductile iron and bronze, red epoxy coatings.

## Strengths and limits

There is no universal material. Stainless is not immune — chlorides pit 304 and stress-crack 316 at temperature; duplex is the answer for chloride-rich water. Carbon steel rusts in wet service and is limited on low temperature by impact toughness. Elastomers set the real temperature ceiling on most soft-seated valves. Galvanic pairs (bronze on stainless, carbon steel on stainless) matter in seawater and mine water.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Exactly what is in the fluid — including trace chlorides, H₂S, solids and pH — and at what temperature?
- Is there a plant piping specification or material class that already fixes body and trim?
- Any sour-service, low-temperature or potable-water certification required?
- Is corrosion or abrasion the dominant wear mechanism? They call for different answers.
- Environment outside the valve: coastal, underground, wash-down, buried?

## Standards

ASTM A216 (WCB), A351 (CF8, CF8M), A105 and A182 (forgings), A352 (LCB), A217 (WC6, WC9), A890 / A995 (duplex castings); API 600 and API 602 (trim numbers); NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 (sour service); ASME B16.34 (pressure–temperature ratings by material group); SANS 241 (drinking water) and related approvals for potable-water-contact materials; FDA 21 CFR 177 for food-contact elastomers.
