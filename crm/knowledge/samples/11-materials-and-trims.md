## What it is

The body material sets the pressure–temperature envelope and corrosion resistance; the **trim** — seats, discs, stems, balls and other wetted moving parts — is what actually wears and seals, and is often a harder or more corrosion-resistant material than the body. Elastomers and plastics appear as seats, liners, sleeves and packing and usually set the temperature limit of the whole valve.

## Typical sizes and ratings

Common body materials and where they fit:

- **Cast iron / ductile iron:** water and low-pressure air, PN 10–16; ductile iron is far tougher and is the water-works standard. Not for steam beyond low pressure, and excluded from hydrocarbons by most plant specifications.
- **Carbon steel** — WCB cast, A105 forged: general process, steam and hydrocarbons to about 425 °C. **LCB / LF2** for low temperature to -46 °C.
- **Chrome-moly** — WC6 / F11, WC9 / F22: high-temperature steam to about 550 °C.
- **Stainless** — CF8 / F304, CF8M / F316: corrosion, food and chemical service, cryogenics. 316 is the default with chlorides or acids.
- **Duplex and super duplex** (F51, F53): seawater, brine, high-chloride mine water.
- **Alloy 20, Hastelloy C, Monel, titanium:** sulphuric, hydrochloric and other aggressive chemistries.
- **Bronze and brass:** small utility valves, potable water, marine.

## Materials

API 600 and API 602 trim numbers are the shorthand for steel valve internals: trim 1 (13 Cr), trim 5 (Stellite seat and disc), trim 8 (13 Cr with hard-faced seats), trim 10 (316), trim 12 (316 with Stellite seats). Hard facing is used above about 400 °C, on steam, and where galling is a risk.

Soft materials: **EPDM** (water, steam to about 120 °C, not oils); **NBR** (oils to about 90 °C); **FKM / Viton** (heat and aggressive chemicals, poor with steam); **natural rubber and polyurethane** (abrasion, slurry sleeves); **PTFE / PFA** (near-universal chemical resistance, seats to about 200 °C); **PEEK** (high-temperature, high-pressure seats).

## Where it is used

- **Water and potable reticulation:** ductile iron, EPDM, potable-water-approved coatings.
- **Mining slurry and tailings:** ductile iron or steel bodies, rubber or polyurethane liners, hard-chromed gates.
- **Petrochemical and refining:** WCB / A105 with 13 Cr or Stellite trim; NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 where H₂S is present.
- **Power generation and steam:** WC6 / WC9 bodies, Stellite trim, graphite packing.
- **Food and beverage, pulp and paper, fire protection:** 316 stainless with FDA-listed elastomers; ductile iron and bronze with red epoxy.

## Strengths and limits

There is no universal material. Chlorides pit 304 and stress-crack 316 at temperature; duplex is the answer for chloride-rich water. Carbon steel rusts in wet service and is limited at low temperature by toughness. Elastomers set the real temperature ceiling on most soft-seated valves. Galvanic pairs (bronze on stainless, carbon steel on stainless) matter in seawater and mine water.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Exactly what is in the fluid — trace chlorides, H₂S, solids, pH — and at what temperature?
- Is there a piping specification or material class that already fixes body and trim?
- Sour-service, low-temperature or potable-water certification required?
- Is corrosion or abrasion the dominant wear mechanism? They call for different answers.

## Standards

ASTM A216 (WCB), A351 (CF8, CF8M), A105 and A182 (forgings), A352 (LCB), A217 (WC6, WC9), A995 (duplex castings); API 600 and API 602 (trim numbers); NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 (sour service); ASME B16.34 (ratings by material group); SANS 241 and related approvals for potable-water contact; FDA 21 CFR 177 for food-contact elastomers.
