## What it is

A butterfly valve is a quarter-turn valve whose disc rotates on a shaft inside the pipe bore. **Resilient-seated (concentric)** valves seal the disc edge against a rubber liner and are the workhorse for water and low-pressure utility service. **Double-offset** valves move the shaft off the disc centreline so the disc lifts away from the seat as it opens, reducing wear. **Triple-offset** valves add a conical metal seat giving bubble-tight shut-off for high temperature and hydrocarbon duty. Body styles are wafer (clamped between flanges), lug (tapped lugs, so it can be an end-of-line valve) and double-flanged for large bores.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- DN 50 to DN 1200 is common; resilient-seated valves to DN 3000 for water works.
- Resilient seated: PN 10 / PN 16, occasionally PN 25.
- Double and triple offset: Class 150 to Class 600, some to Class 900.
- Rubber liners limit temperature to roughly 80–120 °C; triple-offset metal seats exceed 400 °C.

## Materials

Bodies in ductile iron (epoxy coated for water), cast iron, WCB carbon steel, CF8M stainless. Discs in coated ductile iron, aluminium bronze, 316 stainless, duplex. Liners in EPDM (water, mild chemicals), NBR (oils), FKM (heat, aggressive chemicals), PTFE for corrosives. Shafts in 410 or 316 stainless.

## Where it is used

- **Water and wastewater, potable reticulation:** pump station isolation, filter galleries, reservoir outlets — the default large-bore isolation valve; potable duty needs an approved liner and coating.
- **Mining and minerals processing:** process water, air and dust-suppression lines; rubber-lined for light slurry.
- **Petrochemical, refining and power generation:** triple-offset for tight shut-off on hydrocarbons, hot oil and steam; concentric on cooling water.
- **Food and beverage, pulp and paper, fire protection:** stainless, sanitary or PTFE-lined variants; listed wafer or lug valves with supervisory switches.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: light and cheap per unit bore, short face-to-face, easy to automate, usable throttling between about 20 and 70 degrees open. Limits: the disc stays in the flow path (pressure drop, no pigging), liners are damaged by abrasives and heat, and a wafer valve cannot be a dead-end valve without a lug body. Check the disc clears the mating pipe bore on lined or thick-walled pipe.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Wafer, lug or double flanged? Must it hold pressure with the downstream pipe removed?
- Liner material — what is the fluid, and is potable-water approval needed?
- Isolation only, or throttling? Shut-off class required?
- Flange standard and drilling (SANS 1123 table, PN or ASME class)?
- Lever, gearbox or actuator? Above DN 200 a gearbox is usually the minimum.

## Standards

API 609 (lug and wafer, double- and triple-offset), BS 5155 / BS EN 593, ISO 5752 (face-to-face), ASME B16.34, ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), ISO 5211 (actuator mounting), AWWA C504 (rubber-seated water works valves), API 598 (testing).
