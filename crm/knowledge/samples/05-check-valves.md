## What it is

A check (non-return) valve passes flow in one direction and closes automatically when it reverses. It has no operator — the fluid opens it; gravity, a spring or reverse flow closes it. Types: **swing check** (hinged disc, full bore, the traditional choice); **dual-plate wafer check** (two spring-loaded half-discs, short and light); **lift and piston check** (a disc lifts vertically off a seat, globe-style body, good on steam and small bore); **ball check** (tolerant of slurry and viscous fluids); **tilting disc**; and **nozzle / axial check** (spring-loaded, closes very fast to limit water hammer — the "silent" check on pump discharges).

## Typical sizes and ratings

- DN 15 to DN 1200; swing checks to DN 1800 in water works.
- PN 10 / PN 16 cast and ductile iron for water; Class 150 to Class 2500 in steel.
- Dual-plate wafer checks are the compact option from DN 50 to DN 900.
- Limits follow body and seat material, as for isolation valves.

## Materials

Bodies in cast iron, ductile iron, WCB carbon steel, CF8M stainless, bronze, duplex for seawater. Discs in ductile iron, 316 stainless, aluminium bronze. Seats resilient (EPDM, NBR, FKM) for tight shut-off on water and hydrocarbons, metal for steam and high temperature. Springs in Inconel or 316.

## Where it is used

- **Water and wastewater, potable reticulation:** pump discharges — protecting the pump from reverse rotation and the system from drain-back.
- **Mining slurry and tailings:** ball checks and rubber-flap swing checks on slurry pumps; dual-plate on process water.
- **Petrochemical, refining, power generation and steam:** dual-plate and swing checks on process lines; piston and stop-check valves on steam, condensate and boiler feed; nozzle checks on feed pump discharges.
- **Fire protection, food and beverage:** listed swing and wafer checks on sprinkler and pump installations, backflow preventers on the mains; stainless ball and disc checks on product and CIP lines.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: automatic, no power, protects pumps and prevents contamination. Limits: every check leaks slightly unless resilient seated; a slow swing check slams and causes water hammer; a swing check needs horizontal or upward vertical flow; dual-plate valves cannot handle solids. An oversized check chatters and wears — the minimum flow to hold the disc open matters.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What is it protecting, and how fast does flow reverse when the pump stops?
- Horizontal or vertical installation, and which way does flow go?
- Sand, slurry or fibre in the fluid? Then ball or flap check.
- Slight reverse leak acceptable, or tight shut-off required?
- Must a wafer valve fit an existing gap?

## Standards

API 594 (check valves: flanged, lug, wafer and butt-weld), API 6D (pipeline check valves), BS 1868 (steel check valves), BS 5153 (cast iron check valves for water), BS EN 12334, ASME B16.34, ASME B16.10 (face-to-face), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), AWWA C508, API 598 (testing).
