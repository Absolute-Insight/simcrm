## What it is

A ball valve is a quarter-turn isolation valve: a spherical closure with a bore through it rotates 90 degrees between open and closed, sealing against seats on either side. A **floating ball** is held by the seats and pushed onto the downstream seat by line pressure — the usual design in small sizes. A **trunnion-mounted ball** is supported on bearings and the seats are pushed onto the ball instead, keeping torque low in large sizes and high classes. Bodies are one-, two- or three-piece (the three-piece can be serviced in line), with flanged, threaded, socket-weld or butt-weld ends.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- DN 8 to DN 600 routinely; trunnion designs much larger in pipeline service.
- Class 150 to Class 600 covers most plant work; Class 900 to 2500 on trunnion designs.
- Threaded and three-piece valves are often rated by working pressure (for example 1000 psi WOG).
- PTFE seats limit temperature to roughly -20 to +200 °C; metal seats go well beyond.

## Materials

Bodies in WCB carbon steel, CF8M / 316 stainless, brass or bronze for small utility valves, duplex or alloy 20 for corrosives. Balls usually 316 stainless, chrome- or carbide-coated for abrasive duty. Seats in PTFE, reinforced PTFE, PEEK or nylon; metal-to-metal for high temperature or slurry.

## Where it is used

- **Water and potable reticulation:** isolation on pump stations, chlorination skids, building services.
- **Petrochemical and refining:** tight shut-off on hydrocarbon lines and tank farms; fire-safe designs are the norm.
- **Mining and minerals processing:** process water and reagent lines; metal-seated on slurry where a knife gate is too slow.
- **Power generation, steam, food and beverage, fire protection:** metal-seated valves on drains and blowdown; stainless sanitary valves on CIP lines; supervised isolation on sprinkler mains.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: bubble-tight shut-off, low pressure drop in full bore, fast, easy to automate, compact. Limits: not for throttling (seats erode at part-open, though V-port balls exist for control), soft seats limit temperature, abrasive solids score the ball. The body cavity traps fluid — ask about cavity relief on liquids that expand.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Full bore or reduced bore? Does anything need to pass through (pigging)?
- Fluid, temperature, pressure — abrasive, viscous or prone to crystallising?
- Fire-safe, anti-static or fugitive-emissions requirements?
- Lever, gearbox or actuated — and the fail position if actuated?
- End connections and flange standard on the existing pipework?

## Standards

API 608 (metal ball valves), API 6D (pipeline valves), BS 5351, ISO 17292, ASME B16.34 (pressure-temperature ratings), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), ISO 5211 (actuator mounting), API 607 / ISO 10497 (fire testing), API 598 (testing).
