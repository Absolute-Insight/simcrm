## What it is

A first-pass guide matching an industry and service to the valve types most often fitted there. It starts the conversation; it is not a specification — every line still needs fluid, pressure, temperature, size, end connection and actuation confirmed. The product articles carry the detail behind each row.

## Typical sizes and ratings

| Industry | Typical services | Recommended valve types | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining and minerals processing | Process water, reagents, compressed air, dust suppression | Resilient-seated butterfly, ball, gate valves on water | PN 10/16, SANS 1123 Table D/E; ductile iron; magnetic flow meters |
| Mining slurry and tailings | Mill discharge, cyclone feed, thickener underflow, tailings | Sleeved knife gates, pinch valves, ball and flap checks | Rubber or polyurethane liners; full bore; no throttling; pneumatic or hydraulic actuation |
| Water and wastewater | Raw water, sludge, sewage, pump stations | Resilient-seated gate, butterfly, swing and nozzle check; knife gates on sludge | SANS 664 / BS 5163 gates; AWWA C504 butterfly; checks sized against water hammer; electric actuators |
| Potable water reticulation | Bulk mains, reservoirs, district metering | Non-rising-stem resilient gate (buried), butterfly, pressure reducing and air valves | Potable-approved coatings and elastomers; SANS 1529 metering; PN 16 |
| Petrochemical and refining | Hydrocarbons, hot oil, flare, utilities | API 600/602 gate, API 623 globe, API 608 fire-safe ball, triple-offset butterfly, API 594 check, control and API 526 relief valves | ASME Class 150/300; fire-safe certification; NACE for sour service; 3.1 certificates |
| Power generation and steam | Main steam, feedwater, blowdown, drains, cooling water | Parallel slide and pressure-seal gate, Y-pattern globe, piston and stop-check, safety and control valves | Chrome-moly bodies, Stellite trim, butt-weld ends, graphite packing |
| Pulp and paper | Stock, white water, chemicals, steam | Knife gate (stock valves), segmented-ball control valves, stainless ball and butterfly | Fibre packs — avoid wedge gates and dual-plate checks on stock; 316 for bleach chemistry |
| Food and beverage | Product, CIP, steam, water | Sanitary stainless ball and butterfly, hygienic magnetic meters, steam globe and control valves | Polished finishes, FDA elastomers, hygienic connections; stainless actuators for wash-down |
| Fire protection | Sprinkler mains, hydrants, fire pumps | Listed OS&Y gate, supervised lug/wafer butterfly, swing and wafer check, hydrant valves | Listed/approved products only; supervisory switch boxes; grooved or flanged ends |
| Steam (general plant) | Distribution, condensate, reducing stations | Globe, bellows-sealed globe, piston check, pressure-reducing and safety valves | Never a resilient-seated valve on live steam; check the rating at actual temperature |

## Materials

Ductile iron and elastomers dominate water and slurry; carbon steel with 13 Cr or Stellite trim dominates petrochemical and steam; 316 stainless dominates food, pulp and chemical duty. The materials article has the reasoning.

## Where it is used

When a customer opens with "we're a mine" or "it's for a treatment works" and has not named a valve: ask what the fluid is, pick the row, then narrow with the questions in the matching product article.

## Strengths and limits

The table is a generalisation. It misses site standards (many mines and utilities keep approved-product lists), installed base (customers standardise on what their stores hold) and severe-service exceptions — cavitation, cryogenics, oxygen, chlorine — that need engineering input, not a table lookup.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Which industry and plant area — and is there an approved-product list or piping specification?
- What is the fluid, its solids content, pressure and temperature?
- Isolation, non-return, regulation or protection? These are different valves.
- What is already installed, what flange standard is on the pipe, and what power is at the valve?

## Standards
