## What it is

A first-pass guide for matching an industry and service to the valve types most often fitted there. It is a starting point for the conversation, not a specification — every line still needs the fluid, pressure, temperature, size, end connection and actuation confirmed with the customer. The individual product articles carry the detail behind each recommendation.

## Typical sizes and ratings

| Industry | Typical services | Recommended valve types | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining and minerals processing | Process water, reagents, compressed air, dust suppression | Resilient-seated butterfly, ball valves, gate valves on water | PN 10/16, SANS 1123 Table D/E flanges; ductile iron bodies; magnetic flow meters on water balance |
| Mining slurry and tailings | Mill discharge, cyclone feed, thickener underflow, tailings, backfill | Sleeved knife gate valves, pinch valves, ball check and flap check valves | Natural rubber or polyurethane liners; full bore; no throttling; pneumatic or hydraulic actuation; abrasion-resistant magnetic flow meters |
| Water and wastewater | Raw water, sludge, sewage, filter galleries, pump stations | Resilient-seated gate valves, butterfly valves, swing and nozzle check valves, knife gates on sludge and grit | SANS 664 / BS 5163 gate valves; AWWA C504 butterfly; check valves sized against water hammer; electric actuators with SCADA feedback |
| Potable water reticulation | Bulk mains, reservoirs, district metering, building supply | Non-rising-stem resilient gate valves (buried), butterfly valves, pressure reducing valves, air valves | Potable-water-approved coatings and elastomers; SANS 1529 metering; PN 16 standard |
| Petrochemical and refining | Hydrocarbons, hot oil, flare, loading, utilities | API 600/602 gate, API 623 globe, API 608 fire-safe ball, triple-offset butterfly, API 594 check, control valves, API 526 relief valves | ASME B16.5 Class 150/300 flanges; fire-safe and fugitive-emission certification; NACE for sour service; 3.1 certificates |
| Power generation and steam | Main steam, feedwater, blowdown, drains, cooling water | Parallel slide and pressure-seal gate, Y-pattern globe, piston and stop-check valves, safety valves, control valves; butterfly on cooling water | Chrome-moly bodies and Stellite trim on steam; butt-weld ends at high class; graphite packing |
| Pulp and paper | Stock (pulp), white water, chemicals, steam | Knife gate (stock valves), segmented-ball control valves, stainless ball and butterfly, magnetic flow meters | Fibre cuts and packs — avoid wedge gates and dual-plate checks on stock; 316 stainless for bleach chemistry |
| Food and beverage | Product, CIP, steam, water, CO₂ | Sanitary stainless ball and butterfly, hygienic magnetic flow meters, steam globe and control valves, relief valves on boilers and refrigeration | Polished finishes, FDA elastomers, clamp or hygienic connections on product; stainless actuators for wash-down |
| Fire protection | Sprinkler mains, hydrants, fire pumps | Listed OS&Y gate, supervised butterfly (lug/wafer), swing and wafer check, hose and hydrant valves, relief valves on pumps | Listed/approved products only; supervisory switch boxes; grooved and flanged ends; red epoxy coating |
| Steam (general plant) | Distribution, traps, condensate, reducing stations | Globe valves, bellows-sealed globe, piston check, pressure-reducing valves, safety valves | Never a resilient-seated valve on live steam; check body rating at the actual temperature |

## Materials

Read across from the table: ductile iron and elastomers dominate water and slurry; carbon steel with 13 Cr or Stellite trim dominates petrochemical and steam; 316 stainless dominates food, pulp and chemical duty. See the materials article for the reasoning.

## Where it is used

Use the table when a customer opens with "we're a mine" or "it's for a water treatment works" and has not yet named a valve. Ask what the fluid is, pick the row, and then narrow with the questions from the matching product article.

## Strengths and limits

The table is a generalisation. It misses site standards (many mines and utilities have approved-product lists), existing installed base (a customer standardises on what their stores already hold), and severe-service exceptions (cavitation, cryogenics, oxygen, chlorine) that need engineering input rather than a table lookup.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Which industry and which plant area — and is there an approved-product list or piping specification?
- What is the fluid, its solids content, pressure and temperature?
- Isolation, non-return, regulation or protection? These are different valves.
- What is already installed, and what flange standard is on the pipe?
- Manual or automated, and what power is available at the valve?

## Standards

The standards named in each row are expanded in the product articles: API 600, 602, 608, 609, 623, 594, 526; BS 5163; SANS 664, 1123, 1529; AWWA C504; ASME B16.5 and B16.34; ISO 5211; NACE MR0175; and the ISO 9001 / 45001 / 14001 supplier certifications most tenders require.
