## What it is

A flow meter measures the volume or mass of fluid passing a point in a pipe. Two types cover most industrial liquid duty without any obstruction in the bore:

- **Magnetic (electromagnetic) flow meters** apply a magnetic field across the pipe; a conductive liquid moving through the field generates a voltage between two electrodes proportional to velocity. The meter is a lined spool with electrodes and a transmitter. It has no moving parts, no pressure drop and reads slurries as easily as clean water — but the fluid must be conductive (above roughly 5 µS/cm), so hydrocarbons, solvents and demineralised water are out.
- **Ultrasonic flow meters** send sound pulses through the fluid. **Transit-time** meters compare the travel time upstream and downstream, and suit clean or lightly loaded liquids and gases. **Doppler** meters reflect the pulse off particles or bubbles, so they need a dirty fluid. Both come as inline spools or **clamp-on** transducers strapped to the outside of an existing pipe — no shutdown, no cutting — at some cost in accuracy.

Transmitters provide 4–20 mA, pulse, HART and fieldbus outputs, totalising, empty-pipe detection and bidirectional measurement.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- Magnetic: DN 2 to DN 3000; accuracy typically ±0.2–0.5% of reading over a 10:1 or wider range; PN 10 to PN 40, higher on request.
- Ultrasonic inline: DN 25 to DN 3000; clamp-on from about DN 15 upward on most pipe materials.
- Both need straight pipe runs — commonly 5 diameters upstream and 2 downstream for magnetic, more for ultrasonic.
- Process temperature limited by the liner or transducer: PTFE liners to about 180 °C, rubber liners to about 80 °C.

## Materials

Magnetic meter liners in hard rubber (water, sewage), polyurethane (abrasive slurry), PTFE / PFA (chemicals, high temperature), ceramic (severe abrasion). Electrodes in 316L stainless, Hastelloy C, tantalum, platinum or tungsten carbide for abrasive service. Bodies in carbon steel, flanged; stainless for hygienic versions. Ultrasonic transducers in stainless or PEEK.

## Where it is used

- **Water and wastewater, potable reticulation:** bulk water metering, pump station flow, district metering, treatment-works dosing control — magnetic meters are the default; clamp-on ultrasonic for surveys and retrofits.
- **Mining and minerals processing, mining slurry, tailings:** slurry flow to cyclones, thickener underflow, tailings lines — magnetic meters with abrasion-resistant liners and electrodes.
- **Pulp and paper:** stock flow and consistency loops.
- **Food and beverage:** hygienic magnetic meters on product, CIP and water lines.
- **Petrochemical and refining:** transit-time ultrasonic for hydrocarbons (non-conductive, so no magnetic); some custody-transfer designs.
- **Power generation:** cooling water and make-up water flow.

## Strengths and limits

Magnetic: no obstruction, no moving parts, unaffected by density or viscosity, excellent on slurry — but conductive liquids only, a full pipe is required, and electrode coating can be a problem in oily or scaling fluids. Ultrasonic: works on non-conductive fluids, clamp-on needs no shutdown — but accuracy depends on pipe condition, straight runs and fluid cleanliness. Neither measures mass directly; for mass a Coriolis meter is the answer.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What is the fluid, and is it conductive? Any solids, and how abrasive?
- Pipe size, material, lining and available straight run — and can the line be shut down to install a spool?
- Accuracy required, and is it for process control, billing or custody transfer?
- Output needed: 4–20 mA, pulse for a totaliser, HART, Profibus, Modbus?
- Power available at the meter (mains or 24 V DC), and is the transmitter remote or integral? Submersible or buried installation?

## Standards

ISO 6817 and ISO 20456 (electromagnetic flow meters for conductive liquids), ISO 12242 (ultrasonic transit-time meters), OIML R49 and SANS 1529 (water meters for potable cold water, where used for billing), IEC 60529 (IP ratings), IEC 60079 / ATEX (hazardous areas), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), SANAS-accredited calibration where traceability is required.
