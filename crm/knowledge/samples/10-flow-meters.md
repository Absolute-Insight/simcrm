## What it is

A flow meter measures the volume or mass of fluid passing a point in a pipe. Two types cover most industrial liquid duty with nothing in the bore:

- **Magnetic (electromagnetic) flow meters** apply a magnetic field across the pipe; a conductive liquid moving through it generates a voltage between two electrodes proportional to velocity. The meter is a lined spool with electrodes and a transmitter — no moving parts, no pressure drop, and it reads slurry as easily as clean water. The fluid must be conductive (above roughly 5 µS/cm), so hydrocarbons, solvents and demineralised water are out.
- **Ultrasonic flow meters** send sound pulses through the fluid. **Transit-time** meters compare travel time upstream and downstream and suit clean liquids and gases; **Doppler** meters reflect off particles or bubbles and need a dirty fluid. Both come inline or **clamp-on** — strapped to the outside of an existing pipe, no shutdown — at some cost in accuracy.

Transmitters give 4–20 mA, pulse, HART and fieldbus outputs, totalising and empty-pipe detection.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- Magnetic: DN 2 to DN 3000; accuracy typically ±0.2–0.5% of reading; PN 10 to PN 40.
- Ultrasonic inline: DN 25 to DN 3000; clamp-on from about DN 15.
- Straight runs — commonly 5 diameters upstream and 2 downstream for magnetic, more for ultrasonic.
- Temperature set by the liner: PTFE to about 180 °C, rubber to about 80 °C.

## Materials

Liners in hard rubber (water, sewage), polyurethane (abrasive slurry), PTFE / PFA (chemicals, high temperature), ceramic (severe abrasion). Electrodes in 316L, Hastelloy C, tantalum or tungsten carbide for abrasive service. Bodies in carbon steel, flanged; stainless for hygienic versions.

## Where it is used

- **Water and wastewater, potable reticulation:** bulk metering, pump station flow, district metering, dosing control — magnetic meters are the default; clamp-on ultrasonic for surveys and retrofits.
- **Mining slurry, minerals processing and tailings:** slurry to cyclones, thickener underflow, tailings lines — magnetic with abrasion-resistant liners and electrodes.
- **Pulp and paper, food and beverage:** stock flow loops; hygienic magnetic meters on product and CIP lines.
- **Petrochemical, refining and power generation:** transit-time ultrasonic for hydrocarbons (non-conductive, so no magnetic); cooling and make-up water.

## Strengths and limits

Magnetic: no obstruction, no moving parts, unaffected by density or viscosity, excellent on slurry — but conductive liquids only, a full pipe is required, and electrodes coat in oily or scaling fluids. Ultrasonic: works on non-conductive fluids, clamp-on needs no shutdown — but accuracy depends on pipe condition, straight runs and fluid cleanliness. Neither measures mass directly; for mass, a Coriolis meter.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What is the fluid, is it conductive, and how abrasive?
- Pipe size, material, lining and straight run — can the line be shut down to fit a spool?
- Accuracy required: process control, billing or custody transfer?
- Output and power: 4–20 mA, pulse, HART, Modbus; mains or 24 V DC; remote or integral transmitter?

## Standards

ISO 6817 and ISO 20456 (electromagnetic flow meters), ISO 12242 (ultrasonic transit-time meters), OIML R49 and SANS 1529 (water meters for billing), IEC 60529 (IP ratings), IEC 60079 / ATEX (hazardous areas), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), SANAS-accredited calibration where traceability is required.
