## What it is

A gate valve is a multi-turn linear isolation valve: a flat or wedge-shaped gate is lifted out of the flow path by a threaded stem, leaving an unobstructed bore when open. The **solid wedge** is simple and robust; the **flexible wedge** is slit to absorb seat distortion from thermal cycling, favoured on steam; the **parallel slide** uses two spring-loaded discs on high-pressure steam; the **resilient-seated gate** has a rubber-encapsulated wedge sealing on a smooth body bore and is standard in water works. Stems are rising (outside screw and yoke, OS&Y — position is visible) or non-rising (compact, common on buried valves).

## Typical sizes and ratings

- DN 15 to DN 1200; forged small-bore valves to DN 50, cast bodies above.
- Water works resilient-seated: PN 10 / PN 16, some PN 25.
- Steel: Class 150 to Class 2500; pressure-seal bonnets above Class 600 in power service.
- Temperature follows the body: cast iron to about 200 °C, carbon steel to about 425 °C, chrome-moly and stainless higher.

## Materials

Bodies in cast or ductile iron (water, low pressure), WCB carbon steel, WC6 / WC9 chrome-moly (high-temperature steam), CF8M stainless, bronze (small utility). Trims by API 600 trim number — trim 1 (13 Cr), trim 5 (Stellite hard-faced), trim 8 (13 Cr with hard-faced seats), trim 10 (316). Resilient wedges are EPDM or NBR over ductile iron.

## Where it is used

- **Water and wastewater, potable reticulation:** buried mains, reservoir isolation, pump station manifolds — resilient-seated gate valves are the standard.
- **Fire protection:** OS&Y gate valves on sprinkler supplies, where the rising stem proves the valve is open.
- **Petrochemical and refining:** API 600 cast and API 602 forged valves throughout the plant.
- **Power generation and steam:** parallel slide and pressure-seal valves on main steam and feedwater.
- **Mining and minerals processing:** process and tailings return water — not slurry; use a knife gate.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: full bore, very low pressure drop, bidirectional, proven at high pressure and temperature, available in every material and class. Limits: slow to operate, tall when open, not for throttling (the gate chatters and seats erode), and solids settle in the seat pocket. Large valves need a gearbox or actuator and may need a bypass to equalise pressure before opening.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Buried or above ground? Buried valves want non-rising stems, a cap and an extension spindle.
- Does the operator need visible position indication?
- Steam or high temperature? Then flexible wedge or parallel slide, not solid wedge.
- Any solids in the fluid? If so, a gate valve is probably wrong.
- Pressure class, flange drilling and face-to-face — a replacement must match the gap.

## Standards

API 600 (bolted-bonnet steel gate valves), API 602 (compact forged valves), API 603 (corrosion-resistant cast gate valves), BS 1414, BS 5163 / BS EN 1171 (resilient-seated water works gate valves), SANS 664 (cast iron gate valves for water), ASME B16.10 (face-to-face), ASME B16.34, ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges), API 598 (testing).
