## What it is

Two things must be right for a valve to fit and to be safe: its **pressure class** and its **end connection**. The class is the pressure–temperature rating the body carries; the end connection is how it bolts, screws or welds into the line. A valve of the right size and class drilled to the wrong flange table will not fit — and the wrong class on matching flanges is a safety problem.

## Typical sizes and ratings

**ASME / ANSI classes** (150, 300, 600, 900, 1500, 2500) are ratings, not pressures: the allowable pressure depends on material and temperature, tabulated in ASME B16.34 for valves and ASME B16.5 for flanges. Rule of thumb for carbon steel at ambient: Class 150 ≈ 19–20 bar, Class 300 ≈ 50 bar, Class 600 ≈ 100 bar. Ratings fall with temperature — Class 150 is only about 5.5 bar at 400 °C.

**PN ratings** (PN 6, 10, 16, 25, 40, 63, 100) are nominal pressures in bar at ambient, also derated with temperature. PN 16 is the common water-works rating.

**Nominal sizes** are DN (mm) or NPS (inches): DN 50 = NPS 2, DN 100 = NPS 4, DN 200 = NPS 8.

**Face-to-face** dimensions are standardised (ASME B16.10, EN 558 / ISO 5752) so one maker's valve replaces another's — check the series when replacing a valve in an existing gap.

## Materials

A class is only valid for the material on the nameplate. Cast iron valves carry their own lower ratings and must never be used where a steel class was specified.

## Where it is used

Flange standards in South African practice:

- **SANS 1123 (ex SABS 1123):** the local standard, with tables named by pressure in kPa: 1000/3 (Table D), 1600/3 (Table E), 2500/3 (Table F), 4000/3 (Table H). Table D and Table E are the everyday water and mine-service drillings.
- **BS 10** (Tables D, E, F, H): the older British standard SANS 1123 descends from; many drillings interchange, but check.
- **ASME B16.5** (Class 150, 300 and up): petrochemical, refining, power and any plant built to American practice; raised face is normal, RTJ for high pressure.
- **EN 1092-1 / ISO 7005** (PN 10, 16, 25, 40): European-supplied equipment and, increasingly, water works. Table E and PN 16 share drillings in many sizes — not all, so confirm from the tables.

Other ends: **threaded** (BSP to ISO 7 / ISO 228; NPT to ASME B1.20.1 — they do not mate), **socket weld** and **butt weld** (high pressure and steam), **wafer** and **lug** (clamped between flanges), grooved (fire protection), hygienic clamp (food).

## Strengths and limits

Rated classes make valves interchangeable across suppliers, but only inside one system. Mixing a PN 16 valve into a Class 150 line, or a Table D valve into a Table E line, is the commonest site fitting problem — and not always visible until the bolts do not line up.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What flange standard and table or class are the existing flanges? If unknown: bolt count, PCD and hole size settle it.
- Design pressure and temperature — not just operating — so the class can be checked.
- Raised face, flat face or RTJ? Cast iron flat-face flanges must not be bolted to a raised face.
- Face-to-face dimension, if a replacement. Threaded ends: BSP or NPT?

## Standards

ASME B16.34 (valve pressure–temperature ratings), ASME B16.5 and B16.47 (flanges), ASME B16.10 (face-to-face), SANS 1123 (South African pipe flanges), BS 10, EN 1092-1 and ISO 7005 (PN flanges), EN 558 / ISO 5752 (face-to-face), ASME B1.20.1 (NPT), ISO 7 / ISO 228 (BSP), ASME B16.11 and B16.25 (socket-weld and butt-weld ends).
