## What it is

Buyers specify three different kinds of standard, and a good answer on a call keeps them apart:

1. **Product design standards** define how a valve is built, sized and dimensioned — API, ASME, BS / EN, ISO, JIS and SANS documents such as API 600 or ASME B16.34.
2. **Test and performance standards** define what a finished valve must pass — pressure testing (API 598, ISO 5208), fire testing (API 607, API 6FA, ISO 10497), fugitive emissions (ISO 15848, API 641).
3. **Management system certifications** say something about the supplier, not the product:
   - **ISO 9001:2015** — a quality management system: documented, audited processes for how the company designs, sources, makes, inspects and delivers, and corrects what goes wrong.
   - **ISO 45001:2018** — an occupational health and safety management system: hazards to workers and visitors are identified, controlled and reviewed, which is why mines and petrochemical clients ask for it before allowing contractors on site.
   - **ISO 14001:2015** — an environmental management system: the company measures and reduces its environmental impacts and complies with environmental law.

The bodies behind the letters: **ISO** (international); **ANSI** (American national body — "ANSI class" is the everyday name for ASME B16 classes); **ASME** (American mechanical codes — B16 flanges and valves, Section VIII vessels); **API** (American Petroleum Institute — oil and gas valve standards); **BS / EN** (British and harmonised European); **JIS** (Japanese — JIS 10K and 20K flanges on Japanese-built equipment); **SABS / SANS** (South African Bureau of Standards; standards are published as SANS numbers and the SABS mark is a product certification).

## Materials

Traceability is documented under **EN 10204**: a **3.1** certificate is the maker's inspection certificate for the actual material supplied; **3.2** is countersigned by an independent inspector. Petrochemical and power customers usually specify 3.1 for pressure-containing parts.

## Where it is used

- **Petrochemical and refining:** API design and test standards, API 607 fire-safe, NACE MR0175, ISO 15848, 3.1 certificates.
- **Power generation and steam:** ASME codes, material and hydrostatic test certificates.
- **Mining and minerals processing:** SANS flanges, ISO 45001 for site access.
- **Water and potable reticulation:** SANS 1123 flanges, SANS 664 water valves, potable-water approvals for coatings and elastomers.
- **Fire protection, food and beverage:** listed products (FM, UL or SABS approval); FDA / EC 1935 food-contact materials.

## Strengths and limits

A management certification proves a system, not a valve — an ISO 9001 supplier can still ship the wrong product against a wrong specification. A product standard says nothing about delivery or support. Tenders usually need both, and a valve "to API 600" without an API 598 test certificate is incomplete paperwork.

## Questions to ask the customer

- Which design standard and which test standard does the specification call for?
- Material certificates (3.1 or 3.2) required, and for which parts?
- Fire-safe, fugitive-emissions or sour-service requirements?
- Does the site require ISO 45001 or a safety file for delivery? Is the equipment under SANS 347?

## Standards

ISO 9001:2015, ISO 45001:2018, ISO 14001:2015 (management systems); API 598 and ISO 5208 (valve testing); API 607, API 6FA and ISO 10497 (fire testing); ISO 15848 and API 641 (fugitive emissions); NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 (sour service); EN 10204 (material certificates); SANS 347 (pressure equipment); ASME B16.34, ASME B16.5, SANS 1123, EN 1092, JIS B2220 (flanges and ratings).
