## What it is

A pressure relief device protects a vessel, boiler or pipework from overpressure by opening automatically at a set pressure and discharging enough fluid to keep the pressure within the code-allowed accumulation. The terms overlap in daily use, but strictly: a **safety valve** pops fully open on gas or steam; a **relief valve** opens progressively in proportion to overpressure on liquids; a **safety relief valve** does either depending on the application. Designs are **conventional spring-loaded** (back-pressure affects the set point), **balanced bellows** (a bellows isolates the spring from back-pressure — for discharge into a common header) and **pilot-operated** (a small pilot valve controls a large main valve, giving a tight seal right up to set pressure and high capacity in a compact body). A **rupture (bursting) disc** is a non-reclosing alternative, often fitted under a relief valve to isolate it from a corrosive process.

A **pressure reducing valve** is a different device — it regulates downstream pressure in normal operation and offers no protection; do not confuse the two on a call.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- API 526 standardises orifice letters D to T (0.110 to 26 in²) with matching inlet and outlet flange sizes and classes, so a valve from any maker fits the same nozzle.
- Set pressures from about 0.5 bar to several hundred bar; inlet flanges Class 150 to Class 2500.
- Small threaded relief valves for compressors, hydraulic and utility service.
- Capacity is certified, not calculated by the supplier — the nameplate carries the certified capacity and code stamp.

## Materials

Bodies in WCB carbon steel, CF8M stainless, bronze for small utility valves, chrome-moly for high-temperature steam. Nozzles and discs in 316 stainless or Stellite; seats metal-to-metal or soft (FKM, PTFE) for tight sealing near set pressure. Springs in carbon steel, tungsten alloy for high temperature, or Inconel for corrosives. Bellows in Inconel or 316.

## Where it is used

- **Petrochemical and refining:** every pressure vessel, column, exchanger and blocked-in pump discharge; relief into flare headers needs balanced or pilot-operated valves.
- **Power generation and steam:** boiler safety valves (code stamped), superheater and reheater safety valves, deaerator protection.
- **Mining and minerals processing:** compressed air receivers, autoclaves, high-pressure grinding and reagent systems.
- **Water and wastewater:** thermal relief on blocked-in lines, air receivers, surge relief on pump discharges.
- **Food and beverage, pulp and paper:** steam boilers, digesters, CIP heat exchangers, CO₂ and ammonia refrigeration.
- **Fire protection:** pressure relief on fire pump casing and jockey pump discharges.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: self-acting, code-certified protection that needs no power or signal. Limits: it is a last line of defence, not a control device; the set point drifts with back-pressure on conventional valves; simmer and chatter destroy seats if the valve is oversized or the inlet piping drops too much pressure (the 3% rule); and every valve must be periodically tested, resealed and its records kept. Sizing needs the governing overpressure case (fire, blocked outlet, control valve failure), which is engineering work, not a catalogue lookup.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What is being protected, and what code does it fall under (ASME VIII, ASME I boiler, SANS 347 vessel category)?
- Set pressure, allowable overpressure and the required relieving capacity — or the process data to calculate it.
- Fluid state at relief: gas, steam, liquid or two-phase?
- Where does it discharge — to atmosphere or a header with back-pressure?
- Is this a replacement? If so, the existing orifice letter and flange sizes settle most of it.

## Standards

API 520 (sizing and selection), API 526 (flanged steel pressure relief valves — the orifice letters), API 527 (seat tightness), API 576 (inspection), ASME Section VIII and Section I (code stamping UV and V), ISO 4126, BS EN 4126, SANS 347 (pressure equipment regulations categorisation, South Africa), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges).
