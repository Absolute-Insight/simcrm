## What it is

A pressure relief device protects a vessel, boiler or pipework from overpressure by opening automatically at a set pressure and discharging enough fluid to hold the pressure within the code-allowed accumulation. Strictly, a **safety valve** pops fully open on gas or steam, a **relief valve** opens progressively on liquids, and a **safety relief valve** does either. Designs are **conventional spring-loaded** (back-pressure shifts the set point), **balanced bellows** (a bellows isolates the spring from back-pressure — for discharge into a common header) and **pilot-operated** (a small pilot controls a large main valve, sealing tightly right up to set pressure). A **rupture (bursting) disc** is a non-reclosing alternative, often fitted under a relief valve to isolate it from a corrosive process.

A **pressure reducing valve** is a different device — it regulates downstream pressure in normal operation and gives no protection. Keep the two apart on a call.

## Typical sizes and ratings

- API 526 standardises orifice letters D to T with matching inlet and outlet flanges, so valves from different makers fit the same nozzle.
- Set pressures from about 0.5 bar to several hundred bar; inlet flanges Class 150 to Class 2500.
- Small threaded relief valves for compressors, hydraulics and utilities.
- Capacity is certified, not calculated by the supplier — the nameplate carries it with the code stamp.

## Materials

Bodies in WCB carbon steel, CF8M stainless, bronze for small utility valves, chrome-moly for high-temperature steam. Nozzles and discs in 316 or Stellite; seats metal or soft (FKM, PTFE) for tight sealing near set pressure. Springs in carbon steel, tungsten alloy or Inconel; bellows in Inconel or 316.

## Where it is used

- **Petrochemical and refining:** every vessel, column, exchanger and blocked-in pump; relief into flare headers needs balanced or pilot-operated valves.
- **Power generation and steam:** code-stamped boiler, superheater and reheater safety valves.
- **Mining, water and wastewater:** air receivers, autoclaves, thermal relief on blocked-in lines, surge relief on pump discharges.
- **Food and beverage, pulp and paper, fire protection:** steam boilers, digesters, CO₂ and ammonia refrigeration; casing relief on fire pumps.

## Strengths and limits

Strengths: self-acting, code-certified protection needing no power or signal. Limits: a last line of defence, not a control device; conventional valves drift with back-pressure; simmer and chatter destroy seats if the valve is oversized or inlet piping loses too much pressure; every valve needs periodic testing and records. Sizing needs the governing overpressure case (fire, blocked outlet, control valve failure) — engineering work, not a catalogue lookup.

## Questions to ask the customer

- What is protected, and under which code (ASME VIII, ASME I, SANS 347 category)?
- Set pressure, allowable overpressure and required capacity — or the process data to calculate it.
- Fluid state at relief, and discharge to atmosphere or a header with back-pressure?
- Replacement? The existing orifice letter and flange sizes settle most of it.

## Standards

API 520 (sizing and selection), API 526 (flanged steel pressure relief valves), API 527 (seat tightness), API 576 (inspection), ASME Section VIII and Section I (UV and V stamps), ISO 4126, SANS 347 (pressure equipment, South Africa), ASME B16.5 and SANS 1123 (flanges).
