Vectora is a proactive CRM. It watches your pipeline, predicts what is about to
go wrong, and proposes the next action — your day starts with *what needs
attention and why*, not with an empty list.

## The main surfaces

| Surface | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Your numbers at a glance. Reps see what needs them today; managers see how the team is tracking. Every tile clicks through to the records behind it. |
| **Suggestions** (the inbox icon in the sidebar) | The proactive queue: idle deals, missed follow-ups, deals cooling off. Accept a suggestion to act on it, or dismiss it with a reason. |
| **Planner** | Your week as concrete activities. Vectora can propose the week from your suggestion queue; a daily job then matches each planned item against what actually happened. |
| **Leads, Deals, Contacts, Organizations** | The records themselves, in list, kanban and group-by views you can save and share. |
| **Reports** | Built-in reports over the same numbers the dashboard shows, with CSV export, print view, and scheduled email digests. |
| **Mentor, Assistant and Analyst** | Three places to ask a question in plain language, when your administrator enables the model: the Mentor answers how Vectora works, the Assistant answers what your company sells, the Analyst (administrators only) answers how the business is doing from Vectora's own figures. |

## The ideas behind it

- **Proactive, not reactive.** Signals fire *before* a deal is obviously stuck —
  a slowing contact cadence raises a flag before the idle threshold trips.
- **Everything explains itself.** A deal-health score always shows the named
  factors that produced it. Nothing is an unexplained number.
- **You confirm every write.** Suggestions, proposed plans and drafted replies
  never change a record until you review and confirm them.
- **One source of numbers.** Dashboard tiles, forecasts and reports are computed
  in one tested place, so two screens never disagree about the same metric.

product. There is nothing to reach on the internet, and no page here describes
